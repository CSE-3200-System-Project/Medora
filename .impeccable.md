# Medora design context

## Users and purpose

Medora serves patients, doctors, and administrators in a bilingual Bangla/English health-record and appointment workflow. The local OCR annotation workspace serves two research roles: a trained author correcting assisted labels and a licensed clinician or pharmacist producing an independent blinded label and adjudicating disagreements. The primary job is accurate, auditable review with minimal pointer travel.

## Brand and tone

The product should feel clinical, calm, and efficient. It must communicate care and accountability without implying clinical validation. Copy is plain, specific, and cautious. Uncertainty, provenance, consent boundaries, and required human review are always visible.

## Visual direction

- Preserve Medora's restrained green/teal family, light and dark support, and high-contrast text.
- Use a dense editorial workspace for research tools: queue at left, source image in the center, structured labels at right.
- Prefer clear borders, typographic hierarchy, and compact status chips. Avoid gradients, oversized hero text, ornamental cards, and excessive rounding.
- Use system or existing project typography. Numeric identifiers and coordinates use a monospace face.
- Animation is limited to short state transitions and respects reduced-motion preferences.

## Interaction principles

1. Keep image evidence and the field being edited visible together.
2. Make uncertainty, omissions, reviewer role, provenance, and adjudication state explicit.
3. Optimize for keyboard use while retaining accessible labels, focus styles, and touch-sized controls.
4. Autosave locally through the loopback annotation server and show the saved version/time.
5. Blind independent reviewers to model and primary-author output.
6. Never present OCR output as verified clinical data.
